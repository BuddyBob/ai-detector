seeking multiple opinions when making an important decision or choice can help one make a better, more wellinformed decision. there are a few key reasons for this first, different people will have different perspectives based on their own unique experiences. getting advice from more than one person exposes you to a wider range of viewpoints and life lessons that various advisers have gained over time. one person you talk to may focus on an aspect of the situation that another person does not bring up. hearing multiple valid perspectives helps give you a more holistic overview of the choices and factors involved. secondly, talking to multiple people acts as a safeguard against bias. any single individual you ask for advice may have their own subtle or unconscious biases based on their background and relationships that could influence the advice they give. however, getting multiple opinions helps balance out individual biases. common themes or pieces of advice that are consistent across different discussions are likely more objective and remove one persons biases from skewing the decision. disagreement between advisors also highlights where biases may exist. finally, discussing choices with more than one person allows you to sensecheck information and suggestions. if different people focus on similar important factors or suggest comparable courses of action, that validation can increase confidence in those options. conversely, if advice differs significantly between discussions, it highlights areas that need further deliberation or compromise. the process of comparing and contrasting different advice acts as a useful sanitycheck before making a final choice. in summary, seeking advice from multiple trusted sources provides more wellrounded information from diverse perspectives, mitigates individual biases, and allows validation and sensechecking between discussions. this multipronged approach can significantly improve the quality and thoughtfulness of the decision ultimately made.
