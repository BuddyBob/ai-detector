aretha franklins influence on female empowerment in music the queen of soul, aretha franklin, is an icon whose influence goes far beyond music. through her powerful vocals and bold presence she has become the ultimate symbol of female empowerment in music. her impact could be felt not only by her peers but also by generations to comeand it all started with a single song. in  aretha released respect to critical acclaim that would inspire women everywhere to demand respect for themselves and their work. the song was originally written by otis redding however, it was aretha who shaped its message into something different entirely a powerhouse anthem recognized as one of the first feminist anthems ever created. in this one track alone, she managed to capture the spirit of liberation that so many had been striving for during the civil rights movement and womens liberation movement of the s. she channeled this energy into lyrics such as what you want baby i got it what you need do you know i got it? which highlighted both her strength and confidence as well as encouraging other women to find their own power within themselves too. throughout her career, she frequently used music as a tool for social change through topics like civil rights activism, police violence against africanamericans, lgbtq rights advocacy, religious freedom struggles  just some examples among many more issues important to franklin herself  always bringing awareness while presenting them in an accessible way through melodies everyone could relate too. by doing so aretha franklin established herself not only as an incredibly talented artist but also a role model who encouraged others regardless of gender or race identity or any other differences they may have between each other  reminding us that if we join together we can accomplish anything no matter how challenging things get along the way . this ethos became even more pronounced when looking at later tracks such as freeway of love where she sang about being able
