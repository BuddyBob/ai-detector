summer break in many countries is a muchneeded time of rest and relaxation for students and educators alike. for many people, this break acts not just as a chance to take a breather, but a catalyst to explore new ideas, cultures, and opportunities. shortening the summer break would be a disservice to students, particularly those coming from a different country, or those in an education system with short or no winter and spring breaks. one common issue among students from different countries is the lack of a traditional summer holiday. in many countries, students work longer hours during the summer months and are not able to enjoy the freedom and relaxation of a summer break. on the other hand, those who have the luxury of summer break often make time to explore other cultures, visit new places, and pursue interests that are not possible during the school year. all of these experiences contribute to a learning experience that is valuable in its own right, and cutting short summer break would severely limit these experiences for students from different countries. for those in an education system with short or no winter and spring breaks, the summer serves as both a time of rest and renewal. without these additional breaks, students are more likely to suffer from burnout due to the constant pressure of learning and academic expectations. a long summer break allows students to take a step back and relax, enabling them to reenter the school year feeling refreshed and ready to learn. in conclusion, summer break should not be shortened. it is an important opportunity for students from different countries as well as those within the education system who experience short or no winter and spring breaks. a shortened summer break would not only be detrimental to students physical and mental health but would deprive them of invaluable experiences that come from exploring different cultures and places.
