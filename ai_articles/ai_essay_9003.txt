the effects of climate change on the economy are a growing concern for many in todays world. as temperatures continue to rise, so does the cost of goods and services within our global market place. this is due to various changes that have occurred as a result of rising temperatures, such as increases in energy costs associated with shifting weather patterns, along with the burden placed on farmers who must contend with extreme weather events like drought or floods. additionally, natural disasters more frequently occur which leads to increased insurance premiums and can put a strain on citizens finances if their property is damaged or destroyed. further compounding these issues is the fact that businesses may need to invest large amounts of money into technology solutions designed to reduce greenhouse gas emissions which could potentially add an extra layer onto already tight budgets. one example is renewable energy sources such as solar power and wind turbines which require significant upfront investments before any benefit can be seen from their usage. these expenses ultimately come at a price for consumers since it could lead prices of commodities skyrocketing due to increased production costs associated with using nontraditional forms of energy generation over traditional ones like coal or oil. additionally, economies often rely heavily upon tourism dollars however when severe weather events become more common visitors may not feel comfortable taking vacations in areas prone to hurricanes or other violent storms caused by climate change related shifts in temperature patterns. this loss of income would significantly hurt local businesses who depend upon visitor spending as well as putting people out work whose jobs are linked directly or indirectly through this industry sector all leading towards economic contraction rather than growth during times when we should actually be striving for expansion instead. overall, climate change has shown itself time after time again how much its realworld impacts can affect even something seemingly distant from environmental concerns like economics yet still cause major disruption throughout entire
