there has been a longstanding debate on whether children should be taught to compete or cooperate. while some believe that competition fosters a sense of drive and ambition in children, others argue that cooperation is a more important skill for them to learn. in my opinion, both are necessary, but cooperation should be given priority over competition. firstly, learning to cooperate is essential for children as it teaches them empathy and how to work with others. in todays world, teamwork is becoming increasingly important in every aspect of life. whether its school projects or extracurricular activities, children need to learn how to collaborate with others to achieve a common goal. cooperation also teaches children to respect other peoples opinions and ideas, and to compromise when necessary. these skills are crucial in building a harmonious society. on the other hand, competition can be healthy if it is introduced in a positive way. it can motivate children to work harder and strive for excellence. however, when competition becomes too intense, it can lead to negative consequences such as jealousy, low selfesteem, and even cheating. therefore, it is important to teach children how to compete in a positive way, where they learn to respect their opponents and the rules of the game. for instance, in a school setting, children can learn to compete in a positive way by participating in friendly academic contests, sports events, or talent shows. this can help them to develop a sense of pride in their abilities and also learn to handle both winning and losing gracefully. however, it is equally important to teach them that competition is not the only way to succeed, and that sometimes working together can lead to greater success. in conclusion, both competition and cooperation are important skills for children to learn. however, cooperation should be given priority over competition as it teaches children essential life skills such as empathy, teamwork, and compromise. by teaching children to cooperate first, we can create a more harmonious and equitable society, where everyone is valued for their contribution.
