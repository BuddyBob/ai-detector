the beauty of film noir can often be overlooked in todays world, where the emphasis is on big budget blockbusters and the latest special effects. however, there remains a fascination with the classic style of film noir that has been around since its inception in the s. film noir was characterized by dark themes, visuals, and characters that were more complex than those found in traditional hollywood cinema. the genre was marked by its use of heavy shadows to create an atmosphere of mystery and suspense. furthermore, it employed moral ambiguity as a way for audiences to draw their own conclusions about certain characters or situations based on what they observed from limited information presented throughout the movie. one particular example of classic film noir is double indemnity, directed by billy wilder in . this movie tells the story of insurance salesman walter neff fred macmurray whose attraction to femme fatale phyllis dietrichson barbara stanwyck leads him down a path filled with deceit and betrayal as they attempt to commit murderforinsurance money fraud together. throughout this story we see how walters attraction towards phyllis places him at odds with his own morality as he begins making decisions out of desperation instead of reason. we also witness how her manipulation not only affects walter but all those around them who become embroiled in their schemes leading us further down a spiral into darkness and despair full of unexpected twists and turns along the way before ultimately arriving at an ending that leaves us questioning justice itself while still managing to evoke sympathy towards our protagonists despite all their misdeeds. in conclusion, even though modern films may rely heavily on spectacle over substance when it comes
