the use of technology in the classroom has been a contentious issue over the past decade, with many educators arguing that it can be beneficial to students and others warning against its potential for distraction. yet advances in technology have made it increasingly difficult for teachers to ignore the fact that computers and other digital devices are becoming an important part of everyday life. to this end, many schools today have begun incorporating some form of technology into their curricula as a way to prepare students for an increasingly technological world. for instance, interactive whiteboards are often used in place of traditional chalkboards or overhead projectors due to their ability to display notes and visuals from multiple sources at once. this allows teachers to present material in a more engaging manner than ever before, helping keep students interested and engaged throughout a lesson. additionally, using online applications such as google classroom makes it easier for instructors to distribute course materials and assignments electronically, allowing them access both inside and outside of class time with greater ease than ever before. these tools also provide other advantages beyond just increased engagement they can help improve student achievement by enabling individualized instruction tailored towards the needs of each student based on data gathered through assessment programs like iready or nwea map growth tests. in addition, having access to digital resources means that lessons can incorporate multimedia elements such as videos or simulations which can bring concepts alive in ways not possible with traditional learning methods alone. of course there is still much debate about how best these technologies should be integrated into curriculum design without detracting from learning outcomes but one thing is certain if utilized correctly techbased teaching strategies hold tremendous potential for improving educational experiences around the globe now more than ever before
