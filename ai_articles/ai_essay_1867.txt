drivers should not be able to use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle. there are several compelling reasons for this. first and foremost, using a cell phone while driving is distracting and can take a drivers attention away from the road. talking, texting, checking social media or apps  all of these activities require a person to look away from the road and focus on their phone instead. this increases the risk of accidents as the driver is not fully concentrating on their driving. numerous studies have shown that cell phone use while driving can be as dangerous as or more dangerous than drunk driving. secondly, cell phones can enable risky multitasking behaviors while driving. texting requires visual, manual and cognitive attention from the driver as they type messages and read responses. this divides a persons focus between driving and communicating, making it difficult to do both safely at the same time. even handsfree calling still divides attention audibly and cognitively. limiting cell phone use helps discourage distracted driving behaviors. thirdly, prohibiting cell phones reduces the number of potential distractions inside the vehicle. with phones stowed away or powered off, drivers are less tempted to check notifications, scroll through apps or engage in other nondriving activities using their device. this creates a safer driving environment with fewer internal and external distractions competing for a persons focus. in conclusion, the dangers of distracted driving are well documented. allowing unrestricted cell phone use provides unnecessary opportunities for risking lives on the road. for the safety of all drivers and pedestrians, cell phones should not be used by motorists in any way when they are operating a moving vehicle. public safety should take priority over personal communication needs while driving.
