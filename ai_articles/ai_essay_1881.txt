when faced with an important decision, it is wise to get perspectives from more than one person. seeking advice from multiple sources has several benefits. firstly, different people will often see aspects of a situation from different angles. when we get only one opinion, we may fail to consider important factors since no one person thinks of everything. however, hearing multiple viewpoints helps ensure more sides of the issue are brought to light. for example, if trying to pick a university major, talking to a professor, career counselor, and parent all provides varied insights. secondly, people sometimes give biased advice unintentionally based on their own experiences and priorities. talking to just one advisor leaves us open to acting on a recommendation colored by that persons biases. but getting a range of opinions helps balance out these unconscious biases. we can identify common themes in the guidance while disregarding advice faulty or selfinterested. finally, having a variety of perspectives increases the chances of hearing a genuinely useful suggestion. not every advisor will have a silver bullet answer, but the more people we consult, the greater the odds are of uncovering an innovative or creative solution. for instance, if struggling to choose between two jobs, the friend who floated the idea of negotiating for a higher salary at one option could prove pivotal. in conclusion, seeking counsel from multiple trusted sources provides a wellrounded approach to decision making. it helps surface more information, mitigates individual biases, and multiplies our chances of finding an optimal choice. therefore, whenever a consequential choice must be made, the smart path is to gather multiple opinions.
