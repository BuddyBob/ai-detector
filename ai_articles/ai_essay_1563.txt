drivers should not use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle. there are a few key reasons why cell phone use behind the wheel is dangerous and should be avoided. firstly, cell phone use is a major distraction for drivers. talking, texting, or otherwise interacting with a phone requires the drivers visual attention to be directed away from the road. this increases the likelihood of the driver not noticing potential hazards like other vehicles or pedestrians. studies have shown that driving while distracted is as dangerous as driving drunk. the brain needs to focus entirely on the complex task of navigating traffic safely. even handsfree phone conversations siphon away too much mental bandwidth to stay fully aware of ones surroundings. secondly, cell phone use increases reaction time in emergency situations. when a prompt response is needed to avoid an accident, a driver on their phone simply cannot react as quickly as one with their full attention on driving. braking distances and the ability to maneuver are diminished. the extra seconds it takes to process road conditions while distracted can mean the difference between a near miss and a collision. reaction time is a critical factor in avoiding accidents. finally, the act of holding and manipulating a phone physically takes a drivers hands off the wheel. this further impairs control of the vehicle and ability to respond to any rapid change. driving requires having both hands on the wheel to steer as needed. taking a hand off to hold a phone leaves much less control available in split second situations. in summary, cell phone use, even handsfree, introduces too much risk and potential for distraction while driving. the complexity and responsibilities of operating a multiton vehicle on public roads require full attention from drivers at all times. for safety reasons, cell phone activities should be avoided entirely until the vehicle is safely parked. lives could depend on drivers remaining focused only on the road.
