art is a fundamental aspect of the human experience. itspeaks to our emotions, our intellect, and our sense of aesthetics. however, not all art is created equal. some works of art are universally appreciated, while others are only appreciated by niche audiences. this begs the question in order for any work of art to have merit, must it be understandable to most people? in my opinion, the answer is a qualified yes. while not all art needs to be universally understandable, great art should be accessible to a broad audience. in this essay, i will explain why i believe this to be true. first, let us define what we mean by understandable. i do not believe that a work of art needs to be simple or straightforward to be understandable. indeed, some of the greatest works of art are complex and layered, requiring multiple viewings or readings to fully appreciate. however, what i do believe is that a work of art should be relatable to a broad audience. this means that the themes, emotions, and ideas presented in the work should be relevant to most peoples lives. for example, let us consider the film the shawshank redemption. this movie tells the story of a man who is wrongfully accused of murder and sent to prison, where he befriends an older inmate and ultimately escapes. while the details of the story may not be relatable to everyone hopefully!, the themes of hope, redemption, and friendship are universal. most people can relate to the idea of feeling trapped or stuck, and the films message of perseverance and never giving up is inspiring to many. on the other hand, let us consider a work of art that is not relatable to most people. for example, a piece of performance art that involves a person standing still for hours on end while covered in paint. while this may be interesting to some, it is unlikely to resonate with a broad audience. the themes and ideas presented in the piece may be too esoteric or abstract for most people to connect with. of course, there are some works of art that are intentionally niche or avantgarde. these works may be created for a specific audience, or may be meant to challenge our assumptions about what art can be. while i believe that these works can have merit, i do not think that they can be considered great art in the same way that more accessible works can. so why does accessibility matter? firstly, art is a means of communication. if a work of art is not understandable to most people, then it fails to communicate its message effectively. this is not to say that all art needs to have a clear and simple message, but rather that itshould be able to connect with people on some level. if a work of art is too obscure or difficult to understand, then it risks becoming irrelevant or forgotten. secondly, accessibility is important because it allows for a wider audience to appreciate the work. great art should not be limited to an elite few. if a work of art is relatable and accessible, then it has the potential to resonate with people from all walks of life. this allows for a more diverse and inclusive appreciation of art, which benefits everyone. finally, accessibility is important because it allows for art to have a lasting impact. if a work of art is understandable and relatable, then it is more likely to be remembered and celebrated over time. this is why works like to kill a mockingbird or the godfather are still popular and relevant decades after their release. they speak to universal themes and emotions that are timeless and relevant to people of all ages and backgrounds. in conclusion, i believe that for a work of art to have merit, it must be understandable to most people. this does not mean that all art needs to be simple or straightforward, but rather that itshould be relatable and accessible. by creating art that speaks to universal themes and emotions, we can communicate our ideas effectively, connect with a wider audience, and have a lasting impact.
