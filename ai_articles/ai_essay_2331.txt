it is often argued that schools should make students join extracurricular activities, but this can have a damaging effect on students. making students join extracurricular activities increases homework burdens and leads to a heightened level of fatigue and stress. when students are asked to join a club or participate in certain activities, it can impede their ability to focus on their schoolwork. this, in turn, can lead to increased amounts of homework and less time to do it in. this ultimately leaves them with less time to spend on their academic courses and can lead to substandard work. in addition to the increased homework, students may also experience increased levels of fatigue and stress. trying to balance an extracurricular activity with their usual course of study can be taxing and puts students under tremendous stress. this can lead to poorer academic results, as well as mental and physical health issues. with all these factors in mind, principals should not make students join extracurricular activities. the increased strain on a students academic schedule and the strain of anxiety and fatigue can negatively affect a students education. it is up to the students to decide if they would like to join an extracurricular activity, not the school.
