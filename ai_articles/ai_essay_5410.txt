the debate over the use of electronic dictionaries in english learning is a complex one. on one hand, edictionaries are convenient and can help students learn new words quickly. on the other hand, teachers are concerned that overreliance on edictionaries may lead to a decrease in students spelling ability. in my opinion, i agree that the overuse of edictionaries can be detrimental to english learning. to begin with, the use of edictionaries can make students lazy. when students have access to the instantaneous gratification of a dictionary, they may become less motivated to remember the spelling of words. in other words, they may become complacent and rely too heavily on edictionaries. furthermore, the use of edictionaries can lead to a decrease in students vocabulary. while edictionaries can help students learn the definitions of words, they cannot teach students how to use them in context. moreover, edictionaries can be distracting and can take away from learning time. if students are constantly searching for words in an edictionary, they may not be able to focus on the task at hand. in contrast, if students are encouraged to look up words in a physical dictionary, they may become more engaged in the learning process. for example, when students look up words in a physical dictionary, they may spend more time reading and exploring the dictionary, which can lead to a greater understanding of the language. additionally, physical dictionaries can help students learn the correct spelling of words, as they are forced to read the words and find the correct spelling without the help of a computer. in conclusion, while edictionaries can be helpful in certain situations, overreliance on them can be detrimental to english learning. therefore, teachers should encourage students to use physical dictionaries instead of edictionaries and to take the time to learn the correct spelling of words.
