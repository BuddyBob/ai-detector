the debate of whether children should be taught to compete or to cooperate is an ageold question that has no clear answer. some people believe that children should learn to compete in order to develop the skills necessary to succeed in life, while others believe that children should be taught to cooperate in order to foster a sense of community and collaboration. personally, i believe that both competition and cooperation should be taught to children in order to foster their development. competition is a necessary part of life and can be beneficial to a childs development. when children are taught to compete, they learn important skills such as problemsolving, critical thinking, and resilience. competition also teaches children how to set goals, manage their time, and how to handle both success and failure. these skills are invaluable and will serve them well throughout their lives. however, cooperation is also important for children to learn. cooperation teaches children how to work with others, how to share, and how to be respectful of others opinions. it also teaches them how to collaborate and how to be a team player. these skills are essential for success in both their personal and professional lives. in conclusion, i believe that both competition and cooperation should be taught to children in order to foster their development. competition teaches children valuable skills such as problemsolving and resilience, while cooperation teaches them how to collaborate and be a team player. both of these skills are essential for success in life and should be taught to children in equal measure.
