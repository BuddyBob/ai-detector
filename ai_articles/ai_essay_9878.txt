with the everincreasing popularity of streaming services, there has been a revolution in how we as viewers experience television. binge watching a series over a weekend is becoming just as common as tuning into the nightly news. as such, it is important to consider what this new trend means for traditional longform storytelling and its potential impact on our entertainment preferences. the rise of binge watching can largely be attributed to the growing prevalence of streaming services such as netflix, hulu, and amazon prime video. gone are the days when one had to wait an entire week between episodes instead bingers have access to all available seasons or even an entire show at once. this allows us to immerse ourselves in characters lives like never before by skipping right through seemingly mundane scenes and storylines so that we can consume more content in less time. and yet, some may argue that this shift towards binge culture has actually put traditional tv writing at risk because it does not require writers and directors alike to give each episode much thought or detail beyond setting up future plot points  after all why worry about making each installment captivating if viewers will soon move onto another season? consequently, this could lead to longer works consisting mostly of filler material rather than focused story arcs or meaningful character development which would ultimately diminish our appreciation for good writing altogether  particularly among younger generations who rely heavily on streaming platforms for their entertainment needs. that being said, those same people might counter with evidence suggesting that modern audiences prefer short bursts of stories that dont necessarily require intricate planning  after all seeing hundreds or thousands of movies every year doesnt mean you appreciate every single one from beginning till end since most people arent going back again once they finish them but instead opting for something else entirely different! therefore theres clearly room enough within streaming culture both for creative excellence and mass appeal which suggests filmmakers need not worry too much about sacrificing quality in order keep up with demand  indeed
