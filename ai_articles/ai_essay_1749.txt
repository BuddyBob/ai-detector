the use of facial recognition technology like the facial action coding system to read students emotional expressions in the classroom is problematic and an invasion of privacy. while identifying students emotions could provide teachers insight into how engaged or interested their students are, it risks overmonitoring students in a way that could stunt their emotional development and expression. classrooms are meant to be spaces where students can freely explore ideas and learn without constant observation of their reactions. the technology described in the article threatens to transform natural human interactions between teachers and students into something more clinical and impersonal, with a computer system breaking down students facial movements rather than a teacher genuinely connecting with their students. teachers gain true understanding of how their students are doing from building trust and relationships over time, not through hightech surveillance. students should feel comfortable experiencing a full range of emotions in the learning process without having each facial twitch analyzed. discovering ones interests and developing soft skills like collaboration, critical thinking and communication happens through trial and error. constant monitoring may discourage students from taking intellectual risks or make mistakes in front of their peers for fear of having their reactions scrutinized. a relaxed environment is important for students socialemotional growth as well as academic progress. while the technology could provide some useful data to teachers, the risks to student and teacher wellbeing outweigh the potential benefits. education works best when its a partnership between students and teachers, not an emotiondata harvesting exercise. overall, reading students facial expressions without consent through computer programs invades their privacy and could undermine the core purposes of teaching and learning. a less intrusive, more humancentered approach is ideal for supporting students education.
