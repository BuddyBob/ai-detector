despite some potential advantages, a four day school week can present some difficulties for students. for example, students may not have the time to engage in extracurricular activities that would otherwise be beneficial to their development. without a day to practice sports teams, participate in clubs, or get help with homework, certain activities become increasingly difficult to manage. additionally, students looking to make money by working parttime may be held back by reduced hours due to the shorter school week. moreover, students without alternate supervision during an extra nonschool day may have difficulty helping out with younger siblings or other siblings needs due to their own school commitments. while there are potential savings in energy and resources from having shorter weeks, the amount of time that students have to learn new material is reduced as well. with less time spent in class, teachers dont have as much time to dive into difficult concepts, create projects, or provide additional instruction to struggling students. additionally, the catchup that the students have to do after a day away from school can add extra stress to their lives. as an illustration of the issues that students can face with a four day school week, imagine a student who needs to balance playing in the band, helping out with his younger brother, and having a parttime job. having the extra day off from school greatly reduces the amount of time he has to practice and his ability to find time to help his brother with his own homework. the savings in energy and resources from the four day school week is far outweighed by the pressure it can put on students who have further commitments outside of their school day.
