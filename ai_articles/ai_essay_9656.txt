the internet of things iot is a rapidly growing technology that has already infiltrated everyday life. from smart home security systems and networked refrigerators to automated cars, the iot is finding its way into homes and businesses alike. but while the convenience factor when it comes to automation cannot be denied, there are also potentially worrying implications associated with this new wave of connected devices. to ensure that we make the most out of this technology without compromising our safety or privacy, we need to take greater steps in understanding what the iot entails  both from an individual and organizational perspective  as well as exploring potential countermeasures for keeping ourselves secure. at its core, the internet of things consists of physical objects embedded with sensors capable of gathering data about their environment and then sending that information back over a shared network connectionin most cases either wifi or cellular serviceto some other device where it can be analyzed further or used in some unique way such as controlling lights remotely through voice commands. this ability to monitor our surroundings on an unprecedented scale makes it possible for us to automate practically any task imaginable however, taking advantage of these opportunities necessarily requires us to surrender personal information which puts us at risk if those data points fall into malicious hands. because many connected products still lack basic standards like twofactor authentication protocols and encryption safeguards, hackers could gain access relatively easily by exploiting vulnerabilities found in poorly secured networks or inadequate software updates made by manufacturers who fail to patch known flaws in a timely manner. on top of having these weaknesses exposed, another issue arises due largely from interconnectedness itself if one device on an entire system fails due to user error or technical difficulties elsewhere on the same platform, other pieces may cease functioning properly as well resulting in serious damage that might otherwise have been avoided had more careful attention been given up front during setup configuration processes like ensuring all settings were correctly adjusted before activation took place etc. furthermore networks become increasingly vulnerable if users dont regularly change default passwords
