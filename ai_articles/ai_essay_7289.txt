greatness is an attribute that is often associated with individuals who have made significant contributions to society or achieved something remarkable in their respective fields. however, the evaluation of an individuals greatness is a contentious issue. some argue that greatness can only be decided by those who live after them, while others contend that contemporaries are in a better position to judge an individuals greatness. in my opinion, the former view is more tenable, and this essay will provide specific reasons and examples to support this assertion. firstly, contemporary opinions are often influenced by personal biases and prejudices. people tend to judge others based on their preconceived notions and beliefs, which may not reflect the true worth of an individual. for example, during his lifetime, vincent van gogh was often dismissed as a mediocre artist, and his works were not appreciated by his contemporaries. however, today, he is regarded as one of the most influential artists of all time, and his paintings are valued in millions of dollars. thus, the greatness of van gogh could not be accurately assessed by his contemporaries, who were influenced by their personal biases. secondly, the full impact of an individuals contributions may not be immediately apparent. often, it takes time for the true worth of an individuals work to be recognized. for instance, alan turing, the british mathematician, was instrumental in cracking the german enigma code during world war ii, which helped the allies win the war. however, during his lifetime, turing was not appropriately recognized for his contributions and was persecuted for his sexual orientation, which led to his tragic suicide. today, turing is hailed as a hero and is widely regarded as the father of computer science. thus, the greatness of turing could not be adequately assessed by his contemporaries, who failed to recognize the significance of his contributions. thirdly, the evaluation of greatness often requires a historical perspective. a persons impact on society and their lasting legacy can only be appreciated in the context of historical events. for example, mahatma gandhi, the indian nationalist leader, was initially ridiculed and dismissed by the british colonialists and his fellow countrymen. however, today, gandhi is revered as the father of indian independence and is celebrated worldwide for his philosophy of nonviolent resistance. thus, the greatness of gandhi could not be accurately assessed by his contemporaries, who did not have the historical perspective to appreciate his contributions fully. in conclusion, the achievement of greatness is a complex issue that cannot be accurately assessed by contemporaries. personal biases, the timelag in recognizing contributions, and the need for historical perspective all contribute to the difficulty of evaluating an individuals greatness during their lifetime. therefore, it is only those who live after an individual who can make a fair and objective assessment of their greatness.
