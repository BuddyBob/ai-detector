recent studies have proposed that shorter school weeks may provide benefits to students, parents, and teachers alike. the most popular of these proposals is the fourday school week. advantages of the fourday school week include the potential to save money on bussing, electricity, and other operational costs. educators may also find more time to plan quality lessons and have additional days for professional development. furthermore, students may benefit from the extra days off from school for additional extracurricular activities and for more family time. however, a fourday school week is also not without its drawbacks. due to the longer school days, school times may need to be adjusted to accommodate longer hours, leading to impacts on transportation and afterschool activities. also, the switch may adversely affect poor and specialneeds students who rely on regular school schedules. one potential solution to some of the potential disadvantages of a fourday school week is implementing additional academic enrichment activities on the fifth day of the week. this additional day for learning could potentially minimize the impacts of longer school days, while opening up additional opportunities for students to enhance their skills. in addition, providing additional options for transportation and school programs can help to address potential impacts on poor and specialneeds students. ultimately, a fourday school week has the potential to significantly impact the educational experience of students, teachers, and parents. proponents argue that the extra days outside of school can allow for more quality learning opportunities, while detractors argue that the move would create too much disruption to their schedules. nevertheless, further research is needed to determine the implications of the switch for those involved.
