when facing an important decision, it is wise to get perspectives from various people. each person we talk to likely has a slightly different viewpoint based on their own experiences. by consulting multiple sources, we are exposed to more information that we can use to thoughtfully consider our options. friends and family who know us well may provide insights into how each choice aligns with our personal values and priorities. they can help predict how satisfied we may be with each potential outcome. at the same time, their close relationship with us may cause some bias in their opinions. coworkers or classmates have objectivity since they are not as emotionally invested in our decision. they bring fresh perspectives unclouded by personal feelings. however, they may lack understanding of our unique circumstances and constraints. different types of advisors can fill in each others blind spots. a younger friend may see creative solutions an older mentor overlooks. a work peer focused on productivity catches practical concerns a parent prioritizes emotional impact. together their views provide a more nuanced understanding of pros, cons and tradeoffs to weigh. by speaking to a variety assembling various viewpoints, we reduce our vulnerability to any one persons limited experience or biased angle. patterns in the collective counsel point to the choice most solidly reasoned and thoroughly vetted. while the final decision rests with us, input from multiple trusted sources arms us with the clearest picture for deciding what is most aligned with our goals and true needs. in challenging choices, diversity among opinions leads to greater clarity and confidence moving forward.
