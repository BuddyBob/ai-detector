seeking multiple opinions can help someone make a better choice by gaining different perspectives on their decision or problem. while any individual may have their own biases or limitations, hearing the thoughts of several people can provide a more wellrounded view of the options and considerations. some ways that consulting multiple people can aid decision making include getting new information, balancing viewpoints, and reducing doubt. when asking just one person for advice, there is a risk of missing important context or side of the issue that others may catch. by talking to more sources, one is more likely to learn all the key facts before deciding on a course of action. discussing the matter with a variety of individuals can also help balance more extreme or onesided opinions. if only consulting a very optimistic or pessimistic friend, for example, their bias may sway the advice toward only seeing one side of the coin. hearing from people with different emotional dispositions or backgrounds provides the opportunity to consider the issue from multiple angles and arrive at a balanced intermediate perspective. additionally, hearing that others also see merits in the potential choices can reduce doubts and increase confidence in the selection. if every person asked supports a particular decision, then that option seems more justifiably prudent compared to relying only on a single recommendation. the concurrence of multiple friends, family members or colleagues gives validation that the choice is likely informed and prudent rather than leaving lingering doubts about oversighted considerations. in short, the old saying of two heads are better than one rightly acknowledges that getting input from more than a single source can uncover insights that one adviser alone may miss. the diversity of views broadens understanding of situations and leads to decisions made with open eyes to different perspectives rather than misseddata to make an objectively wellreasoned
