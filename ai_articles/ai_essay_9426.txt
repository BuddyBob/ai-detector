the concept of time is something that has perplexed philosophers and scientists for centuries. while it can be argued that the measurement of time is a human construct, its effects are undeniable as each individual experiences them every day. the most fascinating aspect of time lies in its ability to be both finite and infinite at once  despite our efforts to measure it on an everyday basis, we cannot fully comprehend or control what tomorrow brings. times duality is one of the oldest themes explored by literature throughout history from ancient greek myths to modern science fiction stories, writers have used this theme to illustrate the complexity and unpredictability of life itself. perhaps no work better encapsulates how humans struggle with understanding temporal concepts than william shakespeares tragedy macbeth. in this play, macbeth believes he can use his ambition and manipulation skills to bend time in his favor  only to find out at the end that regardless of all his planning, fate had already been set in motion since before he was born. this idea serves as an example for other works which explore similar topics like albert camus the stranger or haruki murakamis kafka on the shore where characters attempt and fail to alter their own fates through extreme action. though these works may offer us some insight into our attempts at controlling time, there remain many questions related to this topic still unanswered such as what purpose does time serve? what caused us humans to measure it so precisely? and why do we always seem so rushed despite having access more hours per day than ever before? answering any single one would require further contemplation but nonetheless provides valuable context when considering just how much power we really have over our lives  even if it only extends so far as being able understand our limited window within infinity.
