while cell phones have become ubiquitous in modern society, using a cell phone while driving can be dangerous and should be avoided. here is why the main priority for a driver should be safely operating their vehicle. however, cell phone use introduces a significant distraction. whenever a driver takes their attention away from the road, even momentarily, to look at or use their phone, it increases the risk of an accident. statistics show that texting while driving makes a collision over twenty times more likely. more broadly, any cell phone use that requires visual, manual, or cognitive attention from the driver is a hazard. a vehicle in motion requires constant awareness of the surrounding traffic environment. road conditions and other vehicles can change rapidly, requiring an attentive driver to react appropriately. however, cell phone use divides a drivers attention and prevents them from dedicating their full focus to the task of driving. numerous studies have shown that phone conversations impact reaction time and compromise focus just as significantly as texting or dialing. partial attention is not enough to safely control a ton or more of fastmoving metal and ensure the protection of both the driver and others on the road. passengers in other vehicles should not be put at risk due to a drivers choice to use a phone. when an accident occurs due to distracted driving, it often harms more than just the atfault driver. innocent people should not face physical or emotional injury because a driver chose to endanger others by diverting focus from the road. restricting cell phone use, except for true handsfree options, is a reasonable precaution aimed at improving public safety for all drivers and pedestrians alike. in summary, the lives and wellbeing of people on the road should take priority over any nonemergency use of a cell phone by a driver. strict limits are justified given the clear safety risks of distracted driving. most experts agree that the only completely safe option is to leave the phone alone until reaching a destination and being parked. for the protection of both themselves and others, drivers operating vehicles should not use cell phones in any capacity. when in control of potentially lethal machinery, full attention to the task of driving must come before any other distracting technology.
