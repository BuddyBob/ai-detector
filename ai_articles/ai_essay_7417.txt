in recent times, the media has become an integral part of our society. it has the power to shape public opinion and influence peoples beliefs. the media is often seen as a watchdog that keeps a check on the government, organizations, and individuals in power. however, with the rise of hour news channels, social media, and the internet, the media has also become a tool for sensationalism, distortion, and manipulation. this has resulted in a situation where it is no longer possible for a society to regard any living man or woman as a hero. in this essay, i will argue that while the media can certainly tarnish the reputation of individuals, it is still possible for society to recognize and appreciate the heroic qualities of people. to begin with, it is true that media scrutiny can often lead to negative publicity and damage the reputation of individuals. for example, in recent years, we have seen several highprofile individuals such as lance armstrong, tiger woods, and bill cosby being accused of various wrongdoings. the media coverage of these cases has been extensive and has led to a significant decline in their public image. such cases highlight the power of the media to shape public opinion and influence our perception of individuals. however, it is important to note that the media is not the only factor that determines the reputation of individuals. there are several other factors such as personal character, achievements, and contributions to society that also play a significant role. for example, there are several individuals who have faced media scrutiny but have still managed to maintain their heroic image. one such example is nelson mandela. despite facing years of imprisonment and intense media scrutiny, mandela is widely regarded as a hero for his contribution to the antiapartheid movement in south africa. moreover, it is important to recognize that heroism is not a static concept. it is a dynamic and evolving concept that changes with time and context. what may have been heroic in the past may not be considered heroic today, and vice versa. for example, someone who was considered a hero in the past may be seen as a villain or a traitor today. similarly, someone who is seen as a hero today may be seen as a villain in the future. this dynamic nature of heroism suggests that while the media can certainly affect peoples perception of individuals, it is not the only factor that determines heroism. furthermore, it is important to recognize that no one is perfect. every individual has flaws and weaknesses, and it is unrealistic to expect anyone to be a perfect hero. however, this does not mean that we should not recognize and appreciate the heroic qualities of individuals. heroism is not about being perfect it is about going above and beyond the call of duty, showing courage in the face of adversity, and making a positive impact on society. these qualities are not diminished by the flaws and weaknesses of individuals. in conclusion, while it is true that media scrutiny can damage the reputation of individuals, it is still possible for society to recognize and appreciate the heroic qualities of people. heroism is a dynamic and evolving concept that changes with time and context. it is not about being perfect, but about going above and beyond the call of duty and making a positive impact on society. as a society, we should strive to recognize and appreciate the heroic qualities of individuals, while also recognizing their flaws and weaknesses. this will help us create a more nuanced and realistic understanding of heroism.
