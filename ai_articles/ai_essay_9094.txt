social media has been a widely debated topic for many years, with some people heralding it as a great resource and others condemning it for its potential to be harmful. but what about the effects of social media on mental health? while there are undeniable positives that come from using these platforms, such as increased connection with friends and family members and access to useful information, studies have found that when used improperly or in excess, social media can cause harm to an individuals psychological wellbeing. one of the most immediately visible risks is increased feelings of loneliness or isolation due to comparing ones life or accomplishments to those posted by peers online this phenomenon is often referred to as fomo fear of missing out. studies have also found correlations between frequent use of social media sites like facebook, twitter and instagram and anxiety levels in users. the more time spent browsing posts made by other people may lead an individual feeling unsatisfied with their own lives  especially if they see images or updates suggesting that everyone else is having a better time than they are. additionally, research has suggested links between excessive amounts of time spent on social networks and depression symptoms. this could relate back either directly as discussed above or through cyberbullying experiences  which appear much more frequently online than offline  creating additional pressure for users who already struggle with selfesteem issues and lead them into further depression states. being exposed so regularly to negative comments about oneself can be damaging enough even without adding any physical bullying actions along with them!. because risk factors associated with social media usage exist alongside the positive aspects mentioned at the beginning of this essay, its important for individuals who choose to use these services frequently understand how their consumption affects their emotions before investing too heavily in networking activities. taking breaks away from screens every now then can help reduce stress while still keeping connections alive through text messages or telephone calls
