there are two schools of thought when it comes to teaching children either to compete or to cooperate. each has its own advantages and disadvantages that must be considered before a decision can be made. on the one hand, teaching children to compete can be beneficial because it instills a sense of healthy competition. this can motivate children to do their best and push themselves to improve. in addition, competition can teach children important life skills such as how to handle disappointment and how to gracefully accept defeat. on the other hand, teaching children to cooperate can also be beneficial because it helps them to develop teamwork skills. these skills are important in both academic and professional settings. in addition, cooperative children often have better social skills and are more likely to empathize with others. ultimately, the decision of whether to teach children to compete or cooperate depends on the individual child and what will work best for him or her. there is no right or wrong answer, and what works for one child may not work for another.
