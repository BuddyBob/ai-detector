drivers should not use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle. there are several important reasons for this. first and foremost, using a cell phone while driving is a serious distraction that puts the driver and others on the road at risk. when a driver is focused on a cell phone conversation or text, their attention is diverted from the critical task of safely navigating traffic. this can easily lead to lapses in awareness of their surroundings and slower reaction times. numerous studies have shown that driving while distracted by a phone is as dangerous as driving drunk. second, cell phone use requires at least one hand to be occupied and takes the drivers eyes off the road. even handsfree conversations or voice commands divert mental focus from driving. operating a vehicle demands the full and undivided attention of both hands on the wheel and eyes on the road ahead. any distraction from a cell phone introduces dangerous possibilities of accidents. third, texting while driving is especially risky because it involves taking both hands off the wheel and eyes completely off the road for prolonged periods. texting drivers are completely unaware of traffic conditions and unable to respond to any sudden dangers. this extreme form of distraction from cell phones has been a cause of many devastating accidents. in conclusion, cell phones present too much of a distraction in any form while a person is responsible for safely operating a vehicle. the risks of accidents and harm are just too high to allow any cell phone use behind the wheel. drivers need their full attention focused on the task of navigating traffic safely without outside distractions. for this reason, cell phone use should not be permitted for drivers. public safety depends on keeping drivers hands on the wheel and eyes on the road.
