drivers should not be able to use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle. there are a few key reasons for this safety is the number one priority when it comes to operating motor vehicles. using a cell phone, whether its talking, texting or other applications, drastically increases distractions from the important task of focusing on the road and other vehicles. numerous studies have shown that drivers who use cell phones are much more likely to be involved in an accident than drivers who do not use phones. this puts not only the driver at risk, but also all other people sharing the road, whether in other vehicles or as pedestrians. even handsfree use of phones has been found to be distracting as a persons mind is occupied with their conversation rather than  focused on driving. legally, many places have already enacted laws against using handheld phones due to the safety risks. however, research shows handsfree use provides little benefit over handheld in terms of reducing crashes. distraction is distraction, no matter how the phone is held. if the goal is truly safety, a full ban on any cell phone use while driving, even handsfree, is most prudent. law enforcement also finds it difficult to distinguish between legal handsfree and illegal handheld use during traffic stops. a total ban simplifies laws and makes them clearer and easier to enforce. as technology advances, phones become more tempting to use with new apps and increased connectivity. it is simply asking too much of a person to divide their attention between an engaging phone and the complex, potentially dangerous task of controlling a fastmoving vehicle. while phones are convenient and many feel they need to be constantly connected, safety should come before any nonessential phone use while driving. lives are at stake with every vehicle on the road. for the wellbeing of all, the most sensible policy is a complete ban on cell phone use when driving.
