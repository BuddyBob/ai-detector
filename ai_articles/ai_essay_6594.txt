facts are the building blocks of knowledge and understanding. if students do not learn the basic facts, they will not be able to understand the concepts. for example, in order to understand the concept of the law of gravity, students must first learn the fact that objects fall at the same rate. if they do not learn this fact, they will not be able to understand the concept. however, once students have learned the basic facts, it is more important for them to understand the concepts than to learn more facts. this is because concepts allow students to see the big picture and to understand how the facts fit together. for example, once students understand the concept of the law of gravity, they can apply it to many different situations, such as why a ball falls to the ground when you drop it. so, while facts are important, concepts are even more important. students should focus on understanding the concepts, and the facts will follow.
