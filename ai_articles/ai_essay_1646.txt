limiting car usage has many benefits for citizens and the environment. according to passage , curbing car usage can help improve air quality and public health. when fewer cars are on the road, there are lower levels of air pollutants like nitrogen oxides, particulate matter, and volatile organic compounds emitted from vehicle exhaust. this leads to cleaner air that is safer to breathe for everyone. passage  notes that air pollution exacerbates respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, so reducing car emissions through limits can help prevent health problems. passage  discusses how limiting car usage reduces traffic congestion and the amount of time wasted stuck in traffic. by choosing alternatives to driving like public transportation, biking, or walking, there will be fewer vehicles competing for road space. this allows traffic to flow more smoothly and improves mobility for those who still need to drive. the passage indicates congestion costs the economy billions annually in lost productivity from commuters spending extra hours in traffic jams. limiting car usage can help alleviate this economic burden. another benefit highlighted in passage  is that curbing car usage leaves more room for other transportation and recreational activities. with less dominance of roads by personal vehicles, public transportation can expand its services and bike lanes as well as multiuse paths can be added. this encourages active transportation that benefits public health. it also opens up city streets previously clogged with traffic to be used for community events and public spaces. this enhances quality of life. in conclusion, limiting car usage produces widespread advantages. it improves air quality, relieves traffic, boosts the economy, and creates more livable cities. while personal vehicles will still have an important role, adopting alternatives even a few days a week can start to shift to a more sustainable transportation system with myriad public benefits. overall, curbing excessive car dependency through limits is a sensible strategy supported by evidence from multiple sources.
