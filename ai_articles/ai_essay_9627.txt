the impact of technology on the music industry is an important yet often overlooked subject. in recent decades, technological advances have made it possible for more people to access music than ever before, while simultaneously undermining the traditional structures that sustained musicians and other creatives in the past. as a result, todays music industry operates very differently from how it did during its heyday in the s and s. for one thing, digital streaming services such as spotify and apple music have become major players in the industry these platforms allow users to listen to entire collections of songs for free or at relatively low cost compared to purchasing albums or singles outright. this has had both positive and negative effects on artists although they may receive higher royalty payments from streaming than salesbased sources like cds or vinyl records, this money is spread out among many different performers due to playlisting algorithms rather than going directly into their pockets. additionally, because these platforms do not require any physical product to be produced e.g., sleeves or shipped as with cds, there are no production costs associated with themso record labels can potentially make more profit even when they pay less per song played by a listener compared with older media formats. meanwhile, online retail outlets such as bandcamp and itunes offer independent artists opportunities previously unavailable through traditional channels anyone can now upload their own recordings directly onto these sites without having first secured a record dealand this means lower overhead costs since there isnt any need for costly studio time or hiring session musiciansvocalistsengineers etc., making selfproducing easier than ever before!
