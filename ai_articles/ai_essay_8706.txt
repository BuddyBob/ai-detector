many people are familiar with the concept of symbolism in literature, but its importance remains underrated. symbolism is a powerful tool that can make any piece of writing relatable and compelling by conveying ideas, emotions, and messages beyond what is simply written on the page. nowhere is this more evident than in harper lees classic novel to kill a mockingbird. the story follows scout finch and her brother jem as they explore their small southern town of maycomb during the s. the novel deals with many adult themes such as racial prejudice, justice versus injustice, gender roles and class inequality  all through the eyes of two children who are still trying to learn about life and themselves. this stark contrast between innocence and experience forms much of the underlying symbolism throughout to kill a mockingbird it serves as a reminder that although life may be difficult at times, it should still be accepted with courage regardless. the mockingbird itself serves as one example atticus finch tells his children never to shoot a mockingbird because its a sin. as readers come to understand later in the book, he does not mean this literally but rather symbolically  for killing an innocent creature is akin to taking away someones innocence or punishing them unjustly for something outside their control which was exactly what happened to tom robinson during his trial for rape when many saw him only as an africanamerican man instead of an individual deserving mercy from societys judgments. . . another symbol present in to kill a mockingbird centres around boo radley initially seen by scout with fear and suspicion due to rumours circulating within maycomb town about him being strange or dangerous  however her perception soon changes after she truly gets to know him better through events such as leaving gifts in knotholes around her house or saving her from bob ewells attack near halloween night the latter even earning boo respect from atticus. by presenting these examples amongst others
