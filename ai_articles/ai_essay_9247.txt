in a world of everincreasing globalization, it has become increasingly important to understand and appreciate the value of cultural diversity. this essay will focus on japanese culture, specifically its traditional cuisine. food is an essential part of any culture and japan is no different. it is estimated that there are over  distinct dishes in japan, each with its own unique flavor profile that comes from the combination of ingredients utilized by chefs throughout the country. a traditional dinner consists of multiple courses including raw fish such as sushi or sashimi, simmered dishes often containing soy sauce or miso, steamed rice and pickles known as tsukemono. an even more elaborate version can include tempura deep fried vegetables or fish, soup made with dashi stock and noodles like soba or udon accompanied by a variety of condiments such as wasabi paste and shoyu a type of soy sauce. the use of seasonings in japanese food reflects both the climate and geography of japans many islands as well as influences from abroad brought about by trade routes over centuries past. the most common base used for sauces is a fermented soy product called shoyu another commonly used seasoning ingredient is mirin which adds sweetness to various dishes. in addition to these two staples, chefs may also use sake rice wine, ginger root, daikon radish, seaweed flakes such as bonito powder or wakame seaweed powder for additional flavoring elements in their creations. japanese cuisine embodies not only flavor but aesthetics too  meals are presented beautifully in order to appeal visually before tasting them at all  this attention to detail gives diners an opportunity to take pleasure not just through taste buds alone but through sight too!
