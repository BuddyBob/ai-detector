there are a few things to consider when answering this question. the first is the age of the child. it is generally accepted that competition is more appropriate for older children, while cooperation is more appropriate for younger children. the second is the context in which the child will be using these skills. if the child is in a competitive environment, such as a sport, then learning to compete may be more important. if the child is in a cooperative environment, such as a classroom, then learning to cooperate may be more important. personally, i believe that children should learn to cooperate. i think this for a few reasons. first, cooperation is a more important life skill than competition. in the real world, we often have to work with others in order to get things done. we have to be able to compromise and communicate effectively in order to be successful. competition, on the other hand, is not as important. sure, there are some jobs that are very competitive, but in most cases, cooperation is more important. second, cooperation is more fun than competition. when we cooperate, we get to help others and feel good about ourselves. when we compete, we often feel stressed and anxious. we may also feel bad about ourselves if we lose. finally, cooperation is more likely to lead to success than competition. when we cooperate, we are working together towards a common goal. everyone is working together and everyone benefits if the goal is met. when we compete, we are often working against each other. someone has to lose in order for someone else to win. in conclusion, i believe that children should learn to cooperate. cooperation is more important than competition, more fun, and more likely to lead to success.
