in recent years, the use of drones in the military and civilian sectors has become increasingly popular. although they have been around for decades, advances in technology and their increased availability to civilians has made them a viable option for many applications. but what exactly are drones, and how do they work?. drones are essentially unmanned aerial vehicles uavs that can fly autonomously or be remotely piloted by an operator on the ground. they come in all shapes and sizes depending on their purpose, from small toylike quadcopters to larger fixedwing models used by military forces. depending on its configuration, a drone may carry cameras or other sensors such as lidar light detection and ranging, infrared thermometers, radar scanners or even weapons systems if used for military purposes. drones can also range from shortdistance hobbyist craft to longrange surveillance uavs capable of global reach. the main components of a drone include onboard batteries providing power electronic control boards with several sensors propellers which allow it to fly gps receivers so it knows where it is going various communication links allowing remote operators to send instructions payload such as cameras or other equipment attached onto the body of the drone itself and motors driving each propeller independently allowing precise movements including hovering in one spot or following predetermined flight paths using autopilot functions programmed into software stored onboard the craft itself. when flying under manual control via remote piloting, an operator uses two joysticks  one controlling altitude while another controls orientation  effectively mimicking actual aircraft operation albeit scaled down considerably due to size constraints not found with full size manned aircraft operating at much higher altitudes than most consumer grade uavs can reasonably achieve safely without colliding into objects below them due mainly to battery life
