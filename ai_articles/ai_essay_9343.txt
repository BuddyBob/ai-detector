the power of music can often be underestimated. many people overlook the incredible impact that a single song or album can have on our lives, but for some it is an integral part of their daytoday existence. for those who suffer from mental health issues such as depression and anxiety, specifically tailored music therapy has been proven to provide relief from symptoms and promote well being. music therapy involves creating a safe space for individuals to explore their emotions through sound and movement whilst providing guidance to help them reach positive outcomes. patient feedback suggests that it allows them to express difficult feelings which they find hard to communicate in more traditional forms of therapy this means that the healing process begins by enabling greater selfawareness before even delving into specific mental health issues themselves. in recent years there have been various studies conducted across different countries in order to assess the efficacy of using music as a form of therapeutic treatment many have found great improvements in both psychological and physiological measures amongst participants after participating in sessions over time. in terms of physiological benefits, studies have shown improvement in respiratory rate, heart rate variability hrv and blood pressure when listening to calming pieces compared with upbeat songs  suggesting that patients are able to better handle stress responses with certain instrumental sounds than without them. . on top of these physical effects, research also indicates that incorporating music into psychotherapy sessions can reduce levels of anger and fear whilst at the same time increasing relaxation levels  suggesting its potential usefulness within contexts such as ptsd recovery programmes or addiction relapse prevention classes etc. furthermore, depending upon the typegenre chosen  i.e., classical versus rock  different qualities may be beneficial classical genres tend towards aiding concentration whereas more upbeat styles actively encourage energy release which might otherwise manifest itself negatively if not expressed constructively through creative outlets like dance or drum circles etc. . . . it is clear then that music plays an important role within mental healthcare by helping sufferers manage painful thoughts  internalised emotions
