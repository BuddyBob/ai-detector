there is no denying that ideas and concepts are more important for students to understand than facts. after all, facts are just pieces of information that have been isolated from their context, while ideas and concepts represent a way of thinking about the world that can be applied to a variety of situations. that being said, there is still a place for facts in education. facts can be seen as the building blocks that are used to construct ideas and concepts. in order to really understand an idea or concept, a student needs to have a firm grasp on the facts that relate to it. for example, consider the concept of photosynthesis. in order to understand how photosynthesis works, a student needs to know a variety of facts, such as what chloroplasts are, what role they play in the process, what light energy is, and how it is used to convert carbon dioxide into glucose. without this basic knowledge, it would be very difficult to understand the concept as a whole. so, while facts are not as important as ideas and concepts, they are still necessary in order for students to gain a deep understanding of the material they are studying.
