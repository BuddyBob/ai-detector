there is no definitive answer to this question as it depends on what is meant by understand ideas and concepts. if it means understanding the underlying principles behind a fact, then i agree that this is more important than simply knowing the fact itself. if, on the other hand, it means understanding every detail of a fact, then i disagree, as there is simply too much information for any one person to know. for example, a student who understands the basic principles of economics will be able to apply those principles to a variety of realworld situations, whereas a student who knows all the specific details of economic theory will be able to do little more than recite those details. similarly, a student who understands the general concepts behind the french revolution will be able to understand the broad strokes of that event, whereas a student who knows all the specific dates and names involved will be able to tell you little more than that. in many cases, it is simply not possible for a student to learn every fact. there are too many facts, and they are changing too rapidly. even if a student could learn all the facts, those facts would be outdated by the time the student graduated. in contrast, if a student understands the principles behind a fact, those principles will still be valid no matter how much the facts change. for these reasons, i believe that it is more important for students to understand ideas and concepts than it is for them to learn facts.
