making mona lisa smile by sarah smith is an interesting article that discusses the development of the facial action coding system, which allows computers to recognize human emotions. the article suggests that this technology can be applied to the classroom in order to read the emotional expressions of students. however, i am not convinced that this is a valuable use of the technology. firstly, it is important to consider the limitations of the facial action coding system. the system can only identify facial expressions, and cannot take into account other factors such as body language or tone of voice. this means that it may not accurately reflect the emotions of the individual, and could lead to false assumptions. furthermore, using this technology to monitor the emotions of students in a classroom could have negative consequences. it may create a sense of surveillance, which could lead to feelings of discomfort and anxiety. additionally, it may result in students trying to hide their emotions or present a false facade, rather than expressing themselves naturally. instead, teachers should focus on developing a positive and supportive classroom environment that encourages open communication and emotional expression. this will allow students to feel comfortable in expressing their emotions and seeking support when needed, without feeling like they are being constantly monitored. in conclusion, while the facial action coding system is an impressive technology, its use in monitoring the emotions of students in a classroom may not be valuable. it is important for teachers to focus on creating a safe and supportive learning environment, rather than relying on technology to read the emotional states of their students.
