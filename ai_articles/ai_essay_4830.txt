the debate between whether children should be taught to compete or cooperate has been going on for some time. on one hand, some people think that competition is essential for children to learn how to strive for excellence and to develop their character. on the other hand, others believe that cooperation is more important for children to learn how to collaborate and work together. in my opinion, both competing and cooperating should be taught to children. competition can be a great motivator for children to strive for excellence. it can teach them how to be persistent and how to never give up. it can also help them develop a sense of pride in their work and the ability to take responsibility for their own actions. for example, if a child is competing against another in a sport, they will be encouraged to practice harder and do their best to be the best they can be. this will help them develop a strong work ethic and the determination to succeed. at the same time, cooperation is also an important skill for children to learn. cooperation teaches children how to collaborate with others, how to respect each others opinions, and how to work together to achieve a common goal. it also teaches them empathy and encourages them to take the perspective of others. for example, if a child is working on a group project, they will be encouraged to work together to complete the task. this will help them develop strong social skills and the ability to work as part of a team. in conclusion, both competing and cooperating should be taught to children. competition can help them develop a strong work ethic and the determination to succeed, while cooperation can help them develop strong social skills and the ability to work as part of a team. teaching children both skills will help them become wellrounded individuals and better prepare them for the challenges of adulthood.
