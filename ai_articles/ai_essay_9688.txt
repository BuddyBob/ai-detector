society and culture. this essay will discuss how contemporary african street art serves as a powerful vehicle for political expression, identity formation, and social commentary in many communities across the continent today. african street art is often created with spray paints or markers on walls, fences, sidewalks, buildings and other public spaces. it provides an accessible platform for those who may not have access to traditional galleries or museums but still wish to engage in artistic expression within their own community. by being visible in public areas such as streets or alleyways it also allows messages to be seen by more people than would otherwise be possible when sharing artwork through conventional means alone. one major way african street artists utilize their craft is to express political views they believe are important while simultaneously encouraging their fellow citizens in activism against injustice or oppression they are facing within society at large. through vivid images that illustrate meaningful messages about current events these works can stimulate discussion among viewers who might not normally consider certain topics related to politics relevant to them personally while also helping raise awareness of broader issues affecting the world beyond ones immediate surroundings. furthermore by using symbols drawn from everyday life
