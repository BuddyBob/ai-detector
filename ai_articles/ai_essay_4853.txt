the debate of whether children should be taught to compete or cooperate has been ongoing for many years. while there are benefits to both approaches, i believe that children should be taught to cooperate. cooperation is a valuable skill that children can use in many aspects of life. learning to cooperate with others teaches children how to work together to achieve common goals. this can be beneficial in the classroom, where children can work together to complete group projects or assignments. it can also be beneficial in the workplace, as it teaches children how to collaborate with colleagues to achieve success. furthermore, cooperation teaches children how to be respectful of others opinions and ideas, which can help them develop strong relationships with their peers. competition, on the other hand, can be beneficial in some cases. it can teach children how to set and achieve goals, as well as how to handle failure. however, it can also lead to negative outcomes such as feelings of envy, resentment, and aggression. this can be especially detrimental in the classroom, where competition can lead to bullying and other forms of mistreatment. in conclusion, i believe that children should be taught to cooperate rather than compete. cooperation teaches children valuable skills such as how to work together to achieve common goals, how to be respectful of others opinions, and how to develop strong relationships with their peers. competition, while beneficial in some cases, can lead to negative outcomes such as envy, resentment, and aggression. therefore, teaching children to cooperate is the best way to ensure that they develop the skills they need to succeed in life.
