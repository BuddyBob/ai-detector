risks due to the still unknown details venus could reveal about the formation of terrestrial planets and evolution of atmospheres. the author provides strong evidence from venuss environment to show why exploration there poses difficulties yet remains important. venus has an atmospheric pressure  times that of earth along with surface temperatures hot enough to melt lead, conditions that would destroy any spacecraft within hours. however, as the article explains, venus remains shrouded in mystery regarding its geological history and the mechanisms that led to runaway greenhouse warming. while harsh conditions persist today, understanding venuss past may give insight into earths climate stability and cast light on earthsized exoplanets recently discovered in habitable zones of their stars. gaining such knowledge through venus exploration could help guide decisions around mitigating climate change back on earth. the challenges of venus exploration are daunting yet surmountable science despite serious risks. in conclusion, the author offers a wellreasoned argument that exploring venus through proposed orbital and potential future surface missions is worth attempting in light of both the scientific value of unlocking the planets mysteries and strategies to mitigate hazards. while venus presents formidable dangers, as the article emphasizes, simply avoiding risks would come at the cost of knowledge. gaining a fuller understanding of geological and atmospheric evolution using venus as a case study could help answer questions central to exoplanet habitability and climate change back on earth. for the potential scientific payoffs, facing the considerable challenges of exploring our closest planetary neighbor merits further consideration and efforts according to the case presented. the author persuasively argues the pursuit of venus science, with prudent risk mitigation, represents a journey worth taking.
