drivers should not use cell phones in any capacity while driving cell phone use while driving has become an epidemic problem on todays roads. with most people never being more than an arms length away from their phone at all times, the temptation to check messages or make calls while behind the wheel is strong. however, using a cell phone in any way when operating a vehicle puts both the driver and others at serious risk. for this reason, drivers should not be permitted to use cell phones in any capacity when driving. numerous studies have shown that cell phone use while driving can impair a person similarly to driving drunk. texting, talking on the phone, or even reaching for a phone to check notifications requires a driver to take their eyes off the road for prolonged periods. this distraction prevents them from properly monitoring traffic conditions, reacting to changing road situations, and increases the likelihood of an accident occurring. a driver engaging in any phone activity is four times more likely to be involved in a crash according to the national highway traffic safety administration. beyond the distraction, cell phone conversations also divert a portion of the brains resources away from focusing solely on driving. having a discussion splits attention between operating the vehicle and actively listening and responding to the other person. even handsfree conversations affect concentration levels and reaction times at a precarious moment when full alertness is required behind the wheel. furthermore, voicetotext or dialing features still require visual attention that takes the drivers eyes off the road. not only is cell phone use dangerous for the driver, but it puts the lives of others at extreme risk as well. thousands are injured or killed every year in accidents related to phone distracted driving. innocent people who share the road, especially pedestrians, cyclists, motorcyclists, and other motorists, did not consent to being vulnerable to a careless driver focused on their cell phone instead of the task of navigating traffic safely. a single lapse in attention can ruin many lives in an instant. the roads simply do not provide room for divided focus and risky behaviors endangering public safety. in conclusion, technology allows constant connectivity but also enables distracting behaviors with serious consequences. as motor vehicles require undivided attention to operate, cell phone use should not be permitted behind the wheel under any circumstances. a full handsfree, eyesontheroad approach is necessary to prioritize safety for all road users. strict laws against phone use and severe penalties for infractions are reasonable to curb a dangerous epidemic and help make the roads safer for everyone.
