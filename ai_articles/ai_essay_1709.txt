picture. no single person can be expected to have considered every angle of a complex situation. getting input from more than one source exposes you to views you might not have otherwise recognized. this helps fill in any blind spots from relying on just one opinion. different people also have varying backgrounds, experiences, and ways of thinking that shape their advice. pulling from a diversity of perspectives helps account for more of what could affect an outcome. others might notice things you overlook. when emotionally invested in our own problems, we can become closeminded or biased. outside people reading the same facts you present may pick up on important details you fail to see due to close involvement. bringing new eyes to the information gives a better chance of catching pertinent parts that could impact the decision. the added insight prevents overlooking something that, had it been noticed, would lead to a different choice. inconsistent advice raises caution flags. if views are in agreement, they provide greater confidence in the path endorsed. but conflicting opinions act as a useful warningdrawing attention to aspects of the situation that inherently contain perspective without realizing it. vetting advice through multiple trusted sources gets closer to the full story by mitigating individual biases or motivations. a diversity of counsel counters the subjective nature of any single recommendation and better ensures a balanced analysis less influenced by personal agendas or preconceptions. in summary, seeking input from more than one trusted
