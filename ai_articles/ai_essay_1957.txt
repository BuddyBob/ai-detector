drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle. there are several good reasons for this. first, cell phone use can be very distracting for drivers. talking, texting, or looking at the screen takes a drivers attention away from the road where it needs to be. this can cause drivers to stop paying attention to their surroundings and not notice important things happening around them like traffic signals, pedestrians, or other vehicles. this kind of distraction can easily lead to accidents. second, using a cell phone means at least one of a drivers hands is not fully on the steering wheel where it belongs. texting requires using both hands briefly which means a driver has no control over the vehicle at all during that time. dialing a number or answering a call also requires at least one hand off the wheel. a driver needs both hands on the wheel to maintain full control of the vehicle at all times. third, studies have shown that cell phone use while driving can impair a persons driving almost as much as being drunk. reaction times and judgment are impaired similar to the effect of alcohol. this kind of impairment puts not only the driver at risk but also passengers and others sharing the road. no one should have to face that kind of risk from other drivers. in conclusion, cell phone use presents too much of a distraction and impairment for safe driving. for the safety of drivers and others on the road, cell phone use should not be allowed for any reason while operating a vehicle in motion. handsfree use does not eliminate the distraction either. the safest policy is for cell phones to be used only when a vehicle is safely stopped.
