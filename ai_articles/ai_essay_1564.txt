while the facial action coding system technology described in the article making mona lisa smile could potentially provide some benefits if used to read students emotional expressions in the classroom, there are also significant risks and ethical concerns with this type of surveillance that outweigh any value. one potential benefit suggested is that teachers may gain insights into how engaged or interested students seem based on their facial expressions. this could help teachers adapt their lessons or methods to better connect with students. however, facial expressions are not always an accurate gauge of a persons true emotions or mental state. students may feel embarrassed having their expressions constantly monitored and analyzed. this could make them selfconscious and less willing to freely participate in class discussions. there are also serious privacy and data protection issues with continuously recording students facial images and expressions without clear consent. facial recognition data is highly sensitive biometric information. if any data was leaked or misused, it could seriously harm students reputations and privacy now or in the future. students deserve to learn in an environment where they feel safe and comfortable expressing themselves naturally without worrying about constant surveillance. overall, while the goals of helping teachers and improving education are reasonable, the risks of normalized biometric surveillance of students are too high. more holistic approaches that consider students wellbeing and do not compromise their basic privacy rights are preferable. techniques like occasional student surveys or interviews could provide feedback to teachers without tracking individuals in such an intrusive way. in many cases, experienced teachers are also capable of gauging student engagement and needs through natural observation alone. unless significantly more careful safeguards and oversight were established, this particular use of facial expression technology in classrooms risks doing more harm than good.
