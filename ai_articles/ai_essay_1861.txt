limiting car usage has several important environmental and public health benefits that all citizens should be aware of. as our passages explain, reducing individual car trips can improve air quality and reduce greenhouse gas emissions that contribute to climate change. when fewer vehicles are on the road each day, there is less pollution from car exhaust being released into the atmosphere. passage a notes that transportation is a major source of air pollutants like nitrogen oxides and particulate matter, which can have serious negative health impacts when inhaled regularly over long periods of time. by driving less and relying more on alternatives like public transit, biking or walking, each person reduces their own contribution to air pollution and the risks that poses for public health on a communitywide level. cutting back on car usage also lessens traffic congestion on city streets according to passage b. with fewer cars clogging up roadways during morning and evening commutes, transit times become more predictable and reliable for buses, as well as for bikers and pedestrians trying to navigate busy intersections. less idling in traffic jams means emissions reductions too. additionally, easing traffic congestion makes streets safer as there is less chance for accidents in highdensity vehicle areas. passage c reiterates this point, noting european cities that have limited car access to downtown cores report fewer traffic incidents once restrictive policies were implemented. aside from environmental and safety benefits, limiting car usage can also save citizens money in the long run as outlined in passage d. by relying less on personal vehicles for transportation needs and embracing alternatives, people spend less on car payments, insurance, gas and maintenance costs over time. that savings can then be put toward other priorities. public transit fares are also typically much more affordable per trip than driving. encouraging citizens to drive less through restricting parking or implementing congestion charges, for example, can shift norms around transportation habits while putting savings back in peoples pockets. in conclusion, as the passages demonstrate, reducing individual car trips and limiting overall car usage provides clear advantages for both the environment and public health of communities. by embracing alternatives and adjusting transportation behaviors, citizens can help improve air quality, alleviate traffic, and potentially save money  all worthwhile goals that deserve consideration and support. increased awareness of these benefits could encourage more widespread adoption of lowcarbon transit options going forward.
