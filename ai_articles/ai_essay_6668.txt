it is often said that young people do not give enough time to helping their communities, however, i would argue that this is not necessarily true. the reality is that young people are incredibly active when it comes to helping their communities, but they do it in different ways than previous generations. firstly, young people are increasingly active in their communities through the use of technology. they are able to connect with each other and organize campaigns, often with the help of social media platforms like facebook, twitter, and instagram. for example, young people have been instrumental in raising awareness about global issues like climate change and poverty. these campaigns often involve online petitions, fundraising drives, and even virtual demonstrations. secondly, young people are increasingly getting involved in volunteer work. while it is true that many young people do not have the same opportunities as previous generations to engage in traditional forms of volunteering, such as working in a soup kitchen or helping out at a local charity, there are still plenty of ways for young people to get involved in their communities. for example, many young people are participating in virtual volunteering, where they can offer their skills, such as web design or writing, to help nonprofit organizations. additionally, many young people are volunteering their time to tutor other students or teach classes at local community centers. finally, young people are increasingly engaging in direct action to help their communities. this often takes the form of participating in protests and other forms of civil disobedience. for example, young people have been at the forefront of the black lives matter movement and have organized numerous demonstrations, rallies, and marches to bring attention to the issues of racial injustice and police brutality. in conclusion, while it is true that young people are not engaging in traditional forms of volunteering as much as previous generations, they are still actively engaged in helping their communities. they are doing so through the use of technology, volunteer work, and direct action. as such, i would argue that young people are still doing their part to contribute to their communities, albeit in different ways.
