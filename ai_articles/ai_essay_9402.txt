the art of falconry has been a popular pastime for centuries. it is the act of catching and keeping wild birds, such as hawks, eagles, falcons or owls to be trained for hunting or sport. falconry was first practiced in asia around bce and spread to europe during the middle ages where it quickly became a favored activity among royalty. to practice falconry requires an immense amount of patience and dedication from both bird and handler. the process begins by obtaining either a wild taken nestling or an older bird that can already hunt with some skill  these are known as passage birds. depending on the age of the bird, handlers will continue training using various techniques until it reaches full maturity and becomes usable for daily hunting expeditions. for centuries people have used different types of equipment when practicing falconry  from bells worn around their ankles to lure prey into traps to hoods made from velvet which are placed over the falcons head so they do not become distracted while flying even gauntlets were sometimes worn by hunters to protect their hands if attacked by larger prey animals like hares or rabbits! not only does this require a great deal of skill but also strength in order to handle such powerful creatures safely. the relationship between man and nature is one that goes back generations  often intertwined with cultural heritage and spirituality too  making it all the more fascinating how those who practice this ancient art form today still enjoy it just as much today as they did hundreds or even thousands years ago! from bonding with your raptor partner through close handling sessions out on walks together  right up til taking them along on actual hunts there is no denying how deeply rewarding this type of pursuit can be when done correctly! and while regulations surrounding licensing requirements vary across countries worldwide, overall sentiment towards modernday falconers remains positive due its commitment towards animal conservation initiatives throughout many parts of europe
