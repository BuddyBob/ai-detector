there is no question that facts are important. after all, they are the foundation upon which concepts and ideas are built. however, i believe that it is more important for students to understand ideas and concepts than to learn facts. here are three reasons why. first, understanding ideas and concepts helps students make connections between different pieces of information. for example, a student who understands the concept of photosynthesis will be better able to understand how the process of photosynthesis affects the food chain. a student who just knows the facts about photosynthesis will be able to recite them, but will not be able to see the connections between them. second, understanding ideas and concepts helps students apply what they have learned to new situations. for example, a student who understands the concept of supply and demand will be able to apply it to a new situation, such as the stock market. a student who just knows the facts about supply and demand will be able to recite them, but will not be able to apply them to a new situation. finally, understanding ideas and concepts helps students remember what they have learned. for example, a student who understands the concept of the scientific method will be better able to remember the steps of the scientific method than a student who just knows the facts about the scientific method. a student who understands the concept of the scientific method will be able to see the connections between the steps and will be able to remember them. in conclusion, i believe that it is more important for students to understand ideas and concepts than to learn facts. understanding ideas and concepts helps students make connections between different pieces of information, apply what they have learned to new situations, and remember what they have learned.
