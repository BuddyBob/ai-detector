drivers should not use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle in todays society, cell phone use while driving has become an epidemic issue. the majority of adults and teenagers own smartphones that are constantly updating and pinging with new notifications. this constant distraction has made cell phone use behind the wheel an extreme hazard. there are several compelling reasons why drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones in any form when operating a motor vehicle. safety should be the top priority while driving. texting, talking on the phone, or even glancing at the phone to check notifications takes a drivers eyes off the road for prolonged periods. numerous studies have shown that driving while distracted is as dangerous as driving drunk. when attention is divided between operating a twoton death machine and a phone conversation or text, reaction time slows and the chance of an accident increases dramatically. even handsfree phone use has been found to be distracting as conversations take cognitive focus away from the complex task of driving. beyond posing a danger to the driver themselves, cell phone use puts others on the road at risk. one moment of distraction could result in an innocent victim being injured or killed in a crash. as vehicles travel at high speeds within feet of each other on roads and highways, a brief lapse in attention can have catastrophic consequences. preventable loss of life should never be a price paid because someone absolutely had to check their social media or respond to a nonemergency text immediately. safety of all motorists and pedestrians must come before convenience or perceived importance of a mobile phone call or message. legally banning cell phone use, except in emergencies, sets a clear boundary that drivers understand is about road safety rather than personal restriction of behavior. with cell phone activities offlimits, focus remains solely on the primary task of navigating vehicles down public roads. distracted driving due to mobile devices has been outlawed in many places already, with the results of reduced crashes and greater enforcement of the ban. prohibiting phone activities establishes responsible driving as the social norm rather than a behavior that most people engage in without thinking about the risks. in conclusion, in light of the overwhelming evidence that cell phones impair driving safety, they should not be used in any capacity while operating a vehicle. lives are too valuable to endanger due to a lax attitude towards distractions behind the wheel or belief that phone activities can be done without compromising attention. a total ban establishes the priority of protecting all road users and helps curb a dangerous epidemic that has real tragic costs. safety must come before convenience when public lives are at stake on our roads.
