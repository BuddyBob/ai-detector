social media has undeniably become a great influence on society in recent years. people from all walks of life and all corners of the globe now have access to an immense amount of information, ideas, and perspectives that were previously out of reach for most individuals. as such, its interesting to consider how social media has specifically impacted young adults sense of selfidentity formation. it is clear that young people today are more exposed than ever before to different images, opinions and values shared by their peers online. through platforms like instagram or facebook they can interact with others who share similar interests or backgrounds while discovering new passions through various accounts they follow. this allows them space to explore different parts of themselves and seek out commonalities between those around them these opportunities werent as readily available even just a decade ago when physical communities were the main source for finding ones identity. thus, it seems fair to say this improved access has been instrumental in aiding many millennials in forming their identities during adolescence. however there is also a darker side associated with this endless feed of content we are now exposed too users often post idealized versions of themselves which can put pressure on those watching from afar leading them down a path towards comparing themselves unfavourably against what might be considered perfection. furthermore, depending on their choice in platformaccounts followed some may only receive reinforcement for certain traits rather than being allowed the freedom to find balance within different aspects that defines who someone fundamentally is e.g., if they follow mainly sport stars online they will likely only see positive messages regarding athleticism. ultimately though social media still offers much potential good so long as individuals dont get lost amongst its surface level offerings  because once you look beyond individual posts its evident there lies beneath true power brought forth by connecting millions together instantaneously  something which should not be overlooked when discussing identity formation
