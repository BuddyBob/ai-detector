planting trees in schools is an incredibly important and beneficial act. on a basic level, trees offer shade, beautify the school grounds, provide privacy for outdoor learning and activities, act as a barrier to reduce noise, and provide habitat for local wildlife. while these individual tangible reasons are important, trees also provide environmental benefits to our local communities. they improve air quality through oxygen production, absorb air pollution and reduce greenhouse gas emissions, and serve as a natural defense against floods, water runoff, and soil erosion. trees are also a lowcost and easy way to create a peaceful and calming atmosphere on campus, allowing for better focus and better learning for students. from a personal standpoint, i have always taken it as part of my duty to protect trees on school grounds. i have experienced firsthand the calming effect of being among the branches and the joy of communing with nature. while many of the environmental benefits of trees in the school are systematic, it is equally important to recognize their ability to improve mental and emotional wellbeing in students, faculty, and the extended community. we have to ensure that trees are respected and protected, so that we and future generations can enjoy their awesome beauty.
