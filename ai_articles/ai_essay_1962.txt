the face on mars a natural landform for decades, the unusual pattern seen on the surface of mars known as the face on mars has fueled speculation that it was sculpted by ancient aliens. however, scientific evidence tells a different story and shows that this landform is a result of natural geological processes rather than artificial construction. the main claim that the face is a natural landform is based on analyses of higher resolution images taken by nasas viking orbiter in the late s. these images revealed that the alleged face is simply a pattern of light and dark patches formed by differences in rock composition and erosion. there are groups of mesas and hills in the area that are bisected by valley networks, creating the overall facial appearance from certain angles. however, up close it is clear that there are no definitive facial features like eyes or a mouth, just irregular rocky outcrops. additional evidence comes from experiments done by nasa scientists to simulate the effects of natural weathering over long periods of time. when they exposed landscape models resembling the cydonia region to conditions similar to mars, patterns reminiscent of the face began to emerge after hundreds or thousands of simulated years. the wind erosion gradually hollowed out darker rock and left lighter colored, harder rock behind  naturally forming strange patterns that resemble recognizable shapes from a distance, like faces or animals. in conclusion, while initial lowresolution images of the face on mars led some to imagine it was sculpted intentionally, higher quality evidence shows that it is clearly a natural rock formation shaped by normal geological processes like erosion. claims of an artificial construct require extraordinary evidence, which has not been found. the face is more reasonably explained as a trick of light and shadow perceived by our patternseeking minds rather than visitation by an ancient alien civilization. for these reasons, the scientific consensus is that mars still retains no evidence of past or present life, artificial or otherwise.
