in recent years, the use of technology in education has become increasingly prominent. as educational institutions are beginning to recognize the importance of integrating digital tools into their curricula, more and more classrooms across america have begun to utilize emerging technologies such as interactive whiteboards, d printing and augmented reality. but despite this shift towards a techinfused learning environment, there is one area where the integration of new methods seems to be lacking student assessment. student assessments play an incredibly important role when it comes to gauging levels of comprehension and understanding within a classroom setting. they allow teachers to collect valuable data that can inform decisions on topics like curriculum structure or lesson plans however, many educators still rely upon traditional paperandpencil tests for evaluating students performance  a method which provides limited information about how well students actually understand course material. technology offers opportunities for far more comprehensive evaluations through online quizzes, automated grading systems and even games that measure certain skillsets such as problem solving. by utilizing these digital solutions, educators can gain detailed insights into individual student progress with much greater accuracy than ever before improving not only the efficiency but also the quality of instruction overall. however due to various factors such as cost or lack of awareness among faculty members  introducing technology into classrooms for assessing students remains something of challenge for many schools. fortunately some initiatives are being taken in order overcome these barriers googles classroom plus program is helping teachers create interactive assessments quickly by leveraging existing tools from g suite while microsoft teams provides similar capabilities via its quiz creator feature . these sorts of measures certainly help reduce upfront costs associated with adoption but they should not replace proper training on how best utilise these emerging technologies without an appropriate level of knowledge regarding effective use cases , any potential benefits could be significantly weakened if not completely lost altogether . . ultimately incorporating modern technologies within student assessment frameworks could enable multiple
