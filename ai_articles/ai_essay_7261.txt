as college students, we are often faced with the question of whether we should take a certain class or not. elective courses offer students the opportunity to explore their interests and passions, but some argue that they should be eliminated in favor of a more structured program. while it may seem like providing clear guidance would be beneficial to students, i disagree that colleges and universities should specify all required courses and eliminate elective courses. one reason why i disagree with this argument is that elective courses can offer students a chance to explore their passions and interests. it is important for students to have the opportunity to learn about the things that they are truly interested in, even if those things are not directly related to their major. for example, a student majoring in biology may have a passion for music and want to take a course in music theory. eliminating elective courses would limit the students ability to explore their interests and could result in a less fulfilling college experience. another reason why i disagree with this argument is that it assumes that all students prefer to follow directions rather than make their own decisions. while some students may prefer a structured program, others may thrive in an environment that allows them to make their own choices. for example, a student who is passionate about writing may want to take a creative writing class, even if it is not required for their major. allowing students to make their own choices can help them develop independence and critical thinking skills. furthermore, specifying all required courses and eliminating elective courses could limit diversity in education. colleges and universities should strive to offer a wide range of courses so that students can be exposed to different perspectives and ideas. eliminating elective courses would limit the number of courses available to students, which could result in a less diverse educational experience. finally, colleges and universities should trust their students to make their own decisions. many college students are adults who are capable of making informed decisions about their education. by providing students with a variety of courses and allowing them to make their own choices, colleges and universities can foster a culture of independence and responsibility. in conclusion, while it may seem like specifying all required courses and eliminating elective courses would provide clear guidance for students, i disagree with this argument. elective courses offer students the opportunity to explore their passions and interests, and limiting these courses could result in a less fulfilling college experience. additionally, assuming that all students prefer to follow directions rather than make their own decisions is misguided, and limiting diversity in education could have negative consequences. ultimately, colleges and universities should trust their students to make responsible decisions about their education.
