the use of facial action coding system facs technology to read student emotions in the classroom is concerning and can undermine the teacherstudent relationship. while identifying emotions may provide teachers insights into how students are feeling, this type of constant surveillance infringes on student privacy and is ultimately counterproductive. facs was designed to analyze facial expressions in a clinical setting, not the classroom. a classroom is meant to be a safe space where students can freely engage in discussion and make mistakes without fear of judgment. constant monitoring of facial cues could intimidate students and make them reluctant to openly participate or take intellectual risks. students should not feel like they are under a microscope and may naturally suppress their true emotions if they know a computer is tracking their every expression. instead of relying on technology, teachers are better able to read student emotions through natural interactions. a caring teacher gets to know each student as an individual and can pick up on subtle behavioral and contextual cues to understand how they are feeling. overt surveillance is unnecessary when a teacher leads with empathy, compassion and respect for student privacy and wellbeing. technology should aim to enhance the learning environment, not replace human relationships or undermine student comfort and trust in the classroom. in summary, while technology that detects emotions aims to provide useful data, its application to constantly monitor students is illadvised and could damage the cooperative studentteacher dynamic that is crucial for learning. a classroom should be a judgementfree place where students feel secure to openly engage, and technology should not compromise this most basic expectation of a positive learning environment.
