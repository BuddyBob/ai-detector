using cell phones while driving should be banned. here is why one of the biggest dangers of cell phone use while driving is distraction. when speaking or texting on a phone, the drivers attention is diverted from the road, increasing the chances of an accident. studies have shown that driving while distracted is as dangerous as drunk driving. however, unlike drunk driving, cell phone use behind the wheel remains legal in most places. phones provide too much of a temptation for drivers. even handsfree use can be distracting as conversations pull attention away from priorities like following traffic laws, checking mirrors, and reacting to unexpected events on the road. accident reports often cite cell phone use as a contributing factor even if the phone was not physically held. a total ban would eliminate this temptation and grey area. reaction times are critically important for safe driving but phone conversations slow response and decision making. drivers talking on phones have been shown to miss traffic signs, not recall crowded areas they drove through, and take longer to brake. these delays, however slight, are dangerous when lives are at stake. the consequences of impaired response time could be tragic. for public safety, cell phone use should be disallowed for drivers. passengers can make calls or texts on behalf of the driver if needed. or drivers can pull over to safely place or receive calls. a full ban would send a clear message that driving requires full focus on the road to avoid accidents in our busy traffic environment. public health and safety must take priority over any perceived convenience of phone access during operation of a vehicle.
