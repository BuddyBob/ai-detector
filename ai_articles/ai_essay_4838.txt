the debate as to whether children should learn to compete or cooperate has been around for a long time. on one hand, some people argue that competition is important in helping children learn to strive and achieve, while others argue that cooperation is key in helping children become better people. in my opinion, i believe children should be taught both cooperation and competition. competition can provide many positive benefits for children. first, it can help instill a sense of ambition and drive within them. this is because when children compete against each other, they are more motivated to do their best and achieve their goals. for example, when children participate in team sports, they can learn the value of striving to achieve success as a collective rather than as an individual. this can help teach them the importance of working together in order to achieve a common goal. furthermore, competition can also help children build their selfesteem and confidence, as they are able to measure their progress and success in comparison to their peers. while competition can be beneficial, teaching children to cooperate is also important. cooperation is key in helping children develop strong interpersonal skills. this is because when children cooperate and work together, they are able to learn how to effectively communicate and collaborate with each other. in addition, cooperation can also help children build relationships with their peers. when they work together, they are able to learn how to respect each others differences and develop positive, supportive relationships with one another. overall, i believe that it is important to teach children both cooperation and competition. competition can help children develop ambition, selfesteem, and confidence, while cooperation can help them build strong interpersonal skills and relationships with their peers. by teaching children both cooperation and competition, they can become better individuals as they grow.
