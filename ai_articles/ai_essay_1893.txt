drivers should not use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle it has become very common for people to use their cell phones while driving. however, doing so poses significant risks and dangers. for safety reasons, drivers should not be permitted to use cell phones in any way when operating a motor vehicle. there are a few key reasons why cell phone use behind the wheel is unsafe and should be prohibited. first, cell phones are a severe distraction for drivers. whether dialing, texting, or looking at the screen, using a phone takes a drivers eyes off the road for extended periods of time. this means they are less aware of their surroundings and less able to anticipate and react to hazards. distracted driving contributes to car accidents on a regular basis. second, cell phones require manual operation with the hands. dialing numbers, scrolling through menus and apps, and typing messages all require hands to be off the wheel. this leaves drivers with only partial control over their vehicle as they navigate traffic. even handsfree features still split a drivers attention between their call and the road. full focus needs to remain on the act of driving itself. third, research shows cell phone use negatively impacts driving ability just as much as being intoxicated. reaction times are slower, judgment is impaired, and focus is divided. this presents as big of a safety risk as drunk driving. drivers have a responsibility to reduce hazards and not endanger others through reckless behaviors like cell phone use behind the wheel. in conclusion, allowing any cell phone operation during driving enables dangerous distractions. the risks of accidents, injuries and loss of life far outweigh any perceived benefits. for the safety of all road users, cell phone use should be banned for drivers so they can keep their full attention on the critical task of navigating vehicles. public safety must take priority over personal convenience.
