the debate over the existence of objective standards for evaluating works of art is a contentious one. on one side, there are those who insist that aesthetic value is subjective, and therefore, there are no standards that can be applied to definitively judge a work of art. on the other side, some posit that standards of judgement can, and should, be established. i believe the latter to be true, and that critics have a responsibility to establish standards for evaluating art, in order to both elevate and protect the arts. a critics main purpose is to assess the quality of a work of art, and to offer an opinion on its merits. without standards, however, a critics opinion is nothing more than an arbitrary judgement. therefore, it becomes difficult to decide what works are worth investing time and money in, or worth exhibiting in museums and galleries. without any standards, the arts become a freeforall, where anything goes. having standards, on the other hand, allows critics to assess works of art in an informed and meaningful way. it allows them to legitimately criticize a work, not just because they dont like it, but because it fails to meet certain standards of excellence. this also allows for some degree of consistency in the way works of art are evaluated, meaning that the same standards can be applied to all works, no matter what their aesthetic. in addition to helping to objectively assess works of art, setting standards also allows for a certain level of protection for the arts. this is because without standards, anyone can declare themselves an artist, regardless of their ability or their knowledge of the history and techniques of the art. having standards helps to ensure that only those who have a true understanding and appreciation of the arts are able to create and exhibit works of art. there are, of course, those who will argue that all standards are arbitrary, and that each artform must have its own set of standards. while this is true to an extent, there are certain underlying principles that apply to all art forms. for example, composition is one of the key elements of visual art, and the same principles can be applied to the composition of music and literature. therefore, while it may be necessary to have specific standards for each art form, there is still room for general standards that can applied to all art. in conclusion, i believe that critics have a responsibility to establish standards for evaluating works of art. not only does this allow for a more informed and meaningful assessment of art, but it also helps to elevate and protect the arts. by setting standards, critics can ensure that only those with a true understanding of the arts are able to create and exhibit works of art.
