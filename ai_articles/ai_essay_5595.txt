in the contemporary world, it is true that employers in many institutions have increasingly paid more attention to degree certificates. however, if this tendency is taken to an extreme, it is easy to lead to the misunderstanding that a degree certificate is the only criterion to measure ones ability and potential in the workplace, which i disagree with. firstly, it is undeniable that a degree certificate can be a reliable indication of ones knowledge and ability in the specialized field. for example, if a job needs a doctorate degree, it is obvious that the employer holds that only those with that degree can be competent for it. therefore, in this case, a degree certificate is certainly a crucial factor in determining whether a person is fit for the job. secondly, employers should also take more factors into account when evaluating a candidates ability and potential. to begin with, not all jobs require a degree certificate. a person with a good sense of service, who is hardworking and can get along well with other colleagues, can be far more suitable for a job than a person who just has a degree certificate. furthermore, ones extracurricular activities can also be a reflection of ones potential. for example, a person who has been serving as a volunteer for several years could have better organizational and communication skills that may be highly relevant for the job. in conclusion, even though a degree certificate can reflect ones academic achievements, itshould not be the only factor to measure ones ability and potential in the workplace. other factors such as experience, extracurricular activities, and interpersonal skills should also be taken into account.
