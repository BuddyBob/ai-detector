drivers should not be able to use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle. there are several reasons for this. first and foremost, using a cell phone while driving can be very distracting and take the drivers attention away from the road. talking, texting, or otherwise interacting with a phone requires looking at and manipulating the device. this takes the drivers eyes and hands off the wheel as well as their mind off focusing on other vehicles and pedestrians. numerous studies have shown that driving while using a phone can impair a driver as much as having a blood alcohol level over the legal limit. second, being distracted with a phone greatly increases the risk of accidents. drivers who use phones are more likely to drift out of their lane, rearend other vehicles, or get into crashes of other kinds. when both hands are on the wheel and full attention is on driving, reaction times are quicker and it is easier to brake, steer away from hazards, or avoid collisions. even brief moments of distraction from a phone could cause a crash or near miss. third, distracted driving endangers more than just the person using the phone. others sharing the road, like passengers, pedestrians, bicyclists and other motorists, are also put at risk by a distracted driver. their safety should not be compromised by someone choosing to use a cell phone. driving is a complex task that requires full focus, and phones are simply too much of a distraction to use while in control of a rapidly moving vehicle. in conclusion, allowing any cell phone use while driving compromises safety and increases crash risks for all road users. drivers should keep both hands on the wheel and their full attention on the road at all times to help avoid preventable accidents. for this reason, driving and cell phone use should remain separate activities.
