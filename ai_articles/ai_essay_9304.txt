when it comes to the topic of sustainable development, few people consider how our daily habits can have a significant impact on helping us achieve global sustainability. as individuals, we may feel that there is little we can do to make a difference but the truth is that small changes in our everyday lives can have positive repercussions for future generations. one such example is by simply reducing our use of plastic and singleuse items  an incredibly easy step to take that has large scale impacts. plastic waste contributes significantly towards the amount of pollution found within our environment, with  million metric tons ending up in the ocean every year according to the guardian . by replacing singleuse items with reusable ones such as coffee cups or water bottles, both money and resources are saved while also reducing plastic waste production. not only this but these new resources are then diverted from potential harmful activities and instead used toward more meaningful projects related to education or medical research which furthers progress towards global sustainability. in addition to considering what materials you buy for your own personal usage, one could also think about how their buying habits could benefit others too something known as conscious consumerism whereby all aspects surrounding an items production process are taken into account when making purchasing decisions good on you . this includes understanding where a product was made and what conditions its workers were living under during its manufacturing process  ensuring no unethical practices such as child labour were involved in order for it be produced. inevitably this requires some research beforehand yet knowing exactly where your products come from allows you not only ensure ethical practices have been followed but also support local businesses wherever possible through fairtrade initiatives among other things which helps promote economic growth across communities while simultaneously aiding global sustainability efforts too. overall it should be clear just how much influence each person has over their consumption patterns regardless of whether they realise it or not
